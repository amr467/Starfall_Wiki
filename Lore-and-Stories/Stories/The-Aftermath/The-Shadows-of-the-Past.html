Chapter 2: Sheela and the Shadows of the Past

The following morning, the grey skies hung low over the ragged landscape, but Figo had no intention of letting V idle. He dragged the youth out to the training grounds—a mud-slicked clearing enclosed by a rotting wooden fence just a short walk from the tavern.

“Well,” Figo grunted, his armored boots sinking into the muck. “If you don’t know your specialty, I bet some practical training will refresh your memory. Every warrior loves his weapon, after all. It’s like a piece of your own soul.”

V glanced at the massive, terrifyingly sharp weapon strapped to Figo’s broad back. “I bet you’ve killed some feisty beasts with that axe.”

Figo froze in his tracks. He turned slowly, his eyes wide with a mix of offense and pure outrage.

“IT’S NOT JUST AN AXE!” Figo roared, his voice shaking the nearby pine trees. “ITS MY WIFE, SHEELA!”

V blinked, utterly paralyzed. He named it what now? he thought, his mind racing to comprehend the sheer absurdity of the statement. What exactly is that weapon to him?!

Before V could utter a word, Figo unstrapped the massive cleaver, brought it gently to his face, and kissed the cold steel. “Don’t let what he said get to you, my sweet. He doesn’t mean it.” He snapped his gaze back to V, glaring fiercely. “SAY SORRY NOW!”

Not wanting to completely ruin his relationship with the only heavily armored vanguard on the team before his first mission, V swallowed his pride and bowed his head. “I’m sorry, Sheela. I didn’t mean to disrespect you.”

Figo grunted, seemingly satisfied, and strapped his “wife” back onto his shoulders.

As they resumed their walk toward the weapon racks, V thought back to the raw panic he had glimpsed in the big man’s eyes during the argument yesterday. “Hey… if you don’t mind me asking,” V began softly, “you’ve actually seen a Dracol before, haven’t you? Would you mind describing it to me? Maybe it will spark something in my memory.”

The moment the word Dracol left V’s lips, Figo visibly shivered. The tough, unyielding exterior of the veteran hunter seemed to crack.

“I… well, since you’re part of the team now, you must know,” Figo said, his voice dropping to a low, grim rumble. “Yes, I’ve seen one. It’s an ancient, catastrophic beast. Luckily, the Hunter’s Guild is always prepared for them… or rather, we were prepared, before the starfall threw the entire order into chaos this past month. You see, they usually emerge in a specific season: mid-summer. Dracols are born from the bellies of dead volcanoes. They feast on destruction, eating and melting anything they cross. Normally, the guild prepares specific cryogenic weapons and specialized hunters to intercept them and minimize the fallout.”

V frowned, looking around at the frost clinging to the mud and the breath pluming from their mouths. “Wait… isn’t it the dead of winter right now?”

“You are indeed correct,” Figo said, his teeth practically chattering—an unsettling sight from a man of his stature. “That’s where the ‘Raging’ part comes in. In incredibly rare, unknown occurrences, a Dracol becomes enraged, breaking its hibernation cycle and keeping it active all year round.”

Figo paused, staring blankly into the distance. “A normal Dracol attacked my village years ago. The guild was there, we were all ready, but our main ballista malfunctioned. The delay… it caused a lot of losses. We weren’t prepared for the sheer heat. This beast is no joke, V. For the unprepared, it is a walking tomb.”

“I’m sorry,” V said, his voice quiet with genuine empathy.

“Not your fault,” Figo muttered, shaking his head to clear the ghosts of his past. He forced a rough clap on V’s shoulder. “Hey, look, we’re at the racks! Let’s get you your fated weapon. From the looks of your build, I’d say you’re either a nimble mage or an assassin. Let’s try the daggers first!”

V felt a sudden surge of excitement, a spark of hope that maybe, just maybe, his muscle memory would take over. “Yeah! Daggers! Let’s do it!”

Two hours later, V was face-down in the dirt.

His muscles burned like fire, his lungs felt like they were filled with ash, and his hands were raw and blistered. He had dropped the daggers a dozen times, tripped over his own feet, and couldn’t even properly pierce a stationary straw dummy.

“I’m done,” V groaned into the mud, battered and entirely defeated. “What is this… am I really this useless?”

Figo stood over him, crossing his arms. The disappointment on his scarred face was painfully obvious. “I don’t get it. What the hell did James see within you? I guess… well, I guess you can be our backpack boy for the time being. Every party needs someone to carry the rations.”

V’s heart sank. A burden. That’s all he was.

“HEY, YOU TWO!”

A sharp, echoing shout cut through the gloomy afternoon air. V pushed himself up on shaking elbows to see James and Trista sprinting toward the training grounds from the direction of the tavern. James was waving a rolled-up piece of parchment wildly in the air.

“GET PACKED!” James yelled, a frantic grin on his face. “WE GOT A NEW QUEST!”

Figo’s eyes lit up the moment James came sprinting into view, parchment clutched in his outstretched fist.

“FINALLY!” Figo bellowed, throwing his arms wide like a man greeting a long-lost friend. “Some content!”

James and Trista skidded to a halt before him, both gasping for breath. Trista pressed a hand to her chest, forcing the words out between ragged inhales.

“We have Dagaruma problems,” she managed, her voice tight with urgency. “A nearby trader sent word—his caravan is under attack. We need to move. Now.”

Figo was already turning toward V, a wide grin splitting his scarred face. “Pack up, lad. It’s time we made some Dagaruma barbecue.”

James, still catching his breath, shot Figo a flat look. “Dagarumas are disgusting,” he said, wrinkling his nose. “They taste like rotten chicken rolled in pig droppings.” A beat of silence followed. His voice dropped to barely a whisper. “…Don’t ask how I know that.”

“Enough!” Trista grabbed both of them by the sleeve and dragged them toward the road with a strength that defied her slight frame. She threw a sharp glance back at V. “You—get the bags from the tavern and meet us at the entrance. Move!”

V nodded and broke into a run.

The tavern’s main hall was already stirring with the restless energy of another grey morning when V shouldered through the door. He had barely crossed the threshold when Ragnik’s graveled voice called out from behind the service counter.

“Hey, V! Off on a mission already, are you?” The old innkeeper’s weathered face crinkled into a warm, knowing smile. “Trista mentioned everything. I hope the road’s good to you—and that memory of yours finds its way home.” He snatched something from beneath the counter and hurled it across the room with a practiced arm.

V caught it instinctively—a tightly wrapped bundle of dried meat, still faintly warm.

“Keep your energy up,” Ragnik said with a firm nod. “Good luck, lad.”

V tucked the pack under his arm, mumbled a grateful thanks, grabbed the party’s travel bags from the storeroom, and made for the door—perhaps a touch faster than his still-aching muscles appreciated.

James was already leaning against the cart when V arrived, arms folded behind his head, looking like a man without a single concern in the world.

“Team Dracol Slayers, move out!” he announced grandly, throwing a fist into the air.

Trista’s eye twitched. “We haven’t even beaten one.”

James turned to her with a patient, serene smile. “Not yet.”

In the background, Figo said nothing. He simply pulled his coat a little tighter and stared hard at the middle distance, jaw set, doing his level best not to visibly shiver.

V wisely said nothing and climbed into the cart.

The horses set off at a hard pace, wheels rattling and churning through the frost-stiffened mud of the road. The bare trees blurred past on either side, the landscape still scarred and grey from the aftermath of the starfall.

“Saving a trader’s caravan,” Figo mused from his seat, with the unmistakable warmth of a man doing something he deeply enjoys. “Bet the pay is solid.” He settled back with a contented sigh. He was, V thought privately, a remarkably simple man. In the best possible way.

V watched the road unspool ahead of them before the question he’d been sitting on finally made its way out.

“So… what exactly is a Dagaruma?”

Trista turned from the path. The look on her face was one V would come to recognize well in the weeks ahead—the particular expression of someone quietly wondering how their life choices had led them to this precise moment. She sighed through her nose and answered anyway.

“Mid-sized monsters. They hunt in packs—aggressive, coordinated, and opportunistic. Since the starfall, they’ve been hitting the trading routes hard.” She laced her fingers together in her lap. “Most of the prey animals they’d normally hunt are dead, and the stardust coating the vegetation has made the plant life toxic to most living creatures. They’re hungry, desperate, and that makes them dangerous. Their primary weapons are their claws and tails—the tails especially. The bite is unpleasant but manageable. The tail is not.”

“Small fry,” Figo added helpfully.

“They cause the most deaths per year of any creature on the guild’s record,” James said, without opening his eyes or adjusting his lounging posture. “Don’t take them lightly.”

A short, loaded silence followed.

“We’re here,” said Trista.

V was first to spot them. About forty paces down the road, four traders stood in a tight circle, spears leveled outward, being slowly encircled by a writhing knot of scaled, low-slung shapes. The creatures moved with a liquid, unsettling patience—feinting left, retreating right, testing for the gap.

“Fun fact,” James announced serenely, stretching his arms above his head as he stepped down from the cart. “The collective noun for a pack of Dagaruma is a trouble waiting to happen.”

Figo had already stopped listening. He unslung Sheela from his back, felt the familiar weight settle into his hands, and rolled his shoulders once.

“Come and get it,” he said quietly—and moved.

For a man sheathed entirely in armor, Figo covered the distance with startling, almost offensive speed. Sheela caught the nearest Dagaruma mid-lunge, the blow landing before the creature even had time to register the threat.

“HA!” Figo wheeled to face the others, chest swelling. “Small fry—what did I say?”

Two of the remaining Dagaruma immediately peeled away from the traders and circled wide to flank him.

Nearby, a pale light had begun to bloom around Trista’s staff as she chanted under her breath, her focus locked entirely on James—who was rocking on his heels with the bright, uncomplicated excitement of a child waiting for his turn at something.

His turn came. He spread his arms wide and spoke.

“Let the dark engulf them.”

The words seemed to press the air flat. A wave of deep shadow unspooled outward like smoke given weight, and everywhere it touched, Dagarumas stiffened, faltered—shrouded in that swirling, lightless dust as though the world had momentarily forgotten they existed.

V stood at the edge of it all, taking in the controlled chaos before him, something bright and unnameable rising in his chest.

So this is what real hunters look like.

A sharp instinct screamed along his spine.

He twisted left—just fast enough. Claws raked the air where his shoulder had been a half-second before. The Dagaruma that had peeled off from the group—the one that had been waiting, hidden, patient—landed hard and spun to face him, coiled and ready.

V scrambled backward, heart hammering, tracking the creature’s tail as it swept in a low, whipping arc—

A knife blurred past his ear and buried itself in the Dagaruma’s throat.

The creature crumpled.

V exhaled.

“Phew.” He looked across to where Figo stood, arm still extended from the throw, expression already cycling back to mild disinterest. “…Thanks.”

Figo grunted. “Watch your flanks.”

When the dust settled, a handful of Dagaruma had cut their losses and bolted into the treeline, a stolen sack of provisions clutched between them. The traders stared after them, then collectively turned to look at the hunters.

“No casualties,” Trista said quietly, already moving toward the nearest injured trader, the healing light blooming soft and steady around her fingertips. She said it like a small, private prayer.

“Thank you.” The trader who spoke looked to be in his mid-thirties—road-worn, steady, the kind of person who had clearly handled bad situations before, though perhaps not quite this bad. He shifted the weight of his enormous pack, one hand still gripping his spear. “You genuinely saved us back there.”

In the background, Figo had found himself a clear patch of ground, dropped to one knee beside the nearest Dagaruma carcass, and was skinning it with the focused, reverent energy of a man who had found his calling.

“The pelts are guild material,” he announced to no one in particular, his voice warm with satisfaction. “These won’t go to waste.”

James returned from a brief, businesslike conversation with the traders’ leader, purse in hand, looking perfectly content with the state of the universe.

“Another job,” Trista said, watching him, her hands still glowing softly as she worked. “…Done well enough, I suppose.”

A second trader stepped forward, pressing his palms together. His eyes moved across all four of them with genuine feeling. “The goods—most of them—will make it to the villages ahead. People who need it badly.” He inclined his head. “May your paths be green, hunters.”

Figo stood up, pelts bundled under one arm, and began loading the cart with brisk efficiency.

“Right,” he said, entirely satisfied with himself and the world. “Let’s move.”
