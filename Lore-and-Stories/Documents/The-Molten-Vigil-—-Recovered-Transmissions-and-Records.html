The Molten Vigil — Recovered Transmissions and Records

The Molten Vigil — Recovered Transmissions and Records

What follows is a collection of transmissions, records, and fragments recovered from or relating to the Molten Imperium and its inner body, the Molten Vigil. They are arranged not chronologically but by what they illuminate — and what, placed beside each other, they suggest.

Not all of these documents were found in the same place. Not all of them were found by the same means. Some were in the outer archive of the Ashen Seat. Some were not.

The collection is incomplete. This is not an editorial choice.

I. Before the Doctrine

~8,000–7,500 BCE — Oral Fragment, Provenance Uncertain

From The Warden Walked Ahead:

In the beginning there was only the ground, and the ground was hot, and the heat was not suffering but instruction.

They had come from somewhere. Nobody remembered where. A being had come — not a man, not a god, something that moved the way fire moves — and the leaving had happened. In the version the archivist prefers, he said only: come, and did not look back.

They followed because something in the way he moved suggested they should.

The elders called him the Warden. The Warden of what was not specified.

— Reconstructed from oral tradition. Third-generation account. The two other known versions differ in ways that cannot be reconciled. Neither has been located.

~7,100 BCE — Trial Record, Witness Examination Notation

From Trial Record — Contested Succession:

Third Trial — Witness Examination
The challenger was examined by the Witness Body in closed session. Duration not recorded. Questions not recorded. The challenger emerged from examination and was confirmed as suitable to lead.

The challenger’s name was removed from this record before filing. This was not standard procedure. The removal was authorized by the Witness Body. No reason is given. The challenger led the Third Community for thirty-one years and was not subsequently challenged. His name does not appear in any other document in the outer archive.

(The marginal note above was added in early Imperial notation — at least two centuries after the document was created — by a hand that does not match any identified archivist. Someone knew the record had been altered. They wrote it down. They did not sign it.)

II. The Body Establishes Itself

~6,200 BCE — Charter of the Permanent Witness Body, Selected Clauses

From Charter of the Permanent Witness Body:

Section III: The Witness Body answers to the doctrine and to the mandate. It does not answer to the leadership council, to the assembled communities, or to any individual leader produced by its own trials.

Section IV: The Witness Body may maintain additional records at its discretion. Such records are the property of the body.

Section V: This charter has no expiration. It may be amended by unanimous agreement of the Witness Body. It may not be amended by any external authority.

Signed by eleven members of the assembled leadership council. Three of the eleven signatories do not appear in any other document in the outer archive before or after this date.

(A body that cannot be questioned, that owns its own records, that cannot be dissolved except by itself. The charter was signed. The leadership council accepted it. What the council understood they were signing is not recorded.)

III. The Empire at Work

~5,500 BCE — Forge Complex Nine, Monthly Production Ledger, Cycle 7

Complete document. Recovered from the outer archive, production records division. One of thousands of identical ledgers from this period. Included here because of the final line.

FORGE COMPLEX NINE — PRODUCTION LEDGER, CYCLE 7
Administered by the Office of Construct Output, Interior Ranges Division

Labor-Class Constructs Produced: 340 units
— Designation L-4 (excavation): 140 units
— Designation L-7 (transport): 120 units
— Designation L-9 (structural assembly): 80 units
All units passed standard activation protocol. Zero defects logged.

Survey-Class Constructs Produced: 47 units
— Designation S-2 (terrain mapping): 31 units
— Designation S-6 (arcane resonance detection): 16 units
All units calibrated to current relay network frequency. Zero defects logged.

Military-Class Constructs Produced: 12 units
— Designation M-3 (perimeter enforcement): 8 units
— Designation M-11 (siege support): 4 units
All units passed combat-readiness assessment. Zero defects logged.

Materials Consumed This Cycle:
Volcanic iron: enough to build approximately forty full-grown horses in solid metal. Arcane resin: 87 standard casks. Relay-grade copper: sufficient to wire a small city block. Binding compounds: 21 barrels. All within standard consumption parameters.

Personnel: 94 forge workers, 12 supervisors, 3 arcane calibration specialists. No personnel incidents this cycle.

Operational anomaly noted: Unit M-11-FOUR developed unsanctioned movement patterns during post-production assessment. Unit was disassembled. Components were reallocated to standard materials pool. No further anomaly.

Next cycle targets: As specified by the Office of Strategic Administration. Ledger submitted for standard review.

(Three hundred ninety-nine constructs produced in one cycle at one forge complex. There were, at this period, twenty-three active forge complexes in the Interior Ranges Division alone. The ledger for the following cycle begins: “Labor-Class Constructs Produced: 347 units. Zero defects logged.” The ledger for the cycle after that begins the same way. The machine did not stop. It did not slow. It did not ask what the constructs were for. Neither did the ledger.)

(M-11-FOUR developed unsanctioned movement patterns. The phrase is not defined anywhere in the outer archive’s procedural documents. It was disassembled. Its components were reallocated. The next line is the target for next cycle.)

~5,100 BCE — Witness Examination Summary, Candidate Designation: R. Vael

Complete document. Recovered from the outer archive, trial records division, Examination Summaries subsection. Examination summaries were filed in the standard archive per Section IV of the founding charter. They are summaries. The full proceedings are held by the body. What follows is the summary.

WITNESS EXAMINATION SUMMARY
Candidate Designation: R. Vael
Trial Cycle: Spring, ~5,100 BCE
Examining Body: Permanent Witness Body, full membership

Physical Trial Result: Pass. Candidate demonstrated exceptional endurance and combat proficiency. Ranked in the top seventh percentile of candidates assessed in the current administrative generation.

Strategic Trial Result: Pass with distinction. Candidate resolved all three logistical scenarios within required parameters. Scenario Three — the contested resource allocation under degraded relay conditions — was resolved using a method not previously observed in this trial cycle. The method has been noted for internal review.

Witness Examination Result: Pass.

Recommendation: Candidate R. Vael is approved for leadership appointment, Third Administrative District, effective immediately upon certification.

Appended Notes:

Candidate has resided at his current posting for four years. Prior to this posting, candidate served in relay station maintenance for eleven years. Candidate has no documented family connections to the existing administrative class.

Candidate demonstrated, during informal observation periods preceding the trial, an interest in astronomical phenomena inconsistent with his stated professional focus. This interest was not raised during the examination. It is noted here for the body’s records.

Candidate’s social contacts over the four-year observation period include three individuals of interest to the body’s ongoing assessment programs. Contact with all three was initiated by the candidate without apparent awareness of their status. The nature of the contacts was assessed as incidental.

Candidate is not aware he has been under observation. He is not to be informed.

(The summary ends there. R. Vael is appointed to the Third Administrative District. He does not appear in the outer archive again for six years, at which point he is referenced in a resource allocation document as having authorized a survey of the northern relay stations. The authorization is within his administrative remit. The survey was conducted. Its findings are not in the outer archive.)

(The method Candidate Vael used to resolve Scenario Three — noted for internal review — does not appear in any subsequent procedural document. What the internal review found is not in the outer archive. Whether R. Vael knew that the body found his method interesting enough to note is not established.)

(The body passed him. The body watched him. These are not contradictory actions.)

IV. What the Body Knew

The Molten Vigil’s private astronomical records have not been recovered. What follows is reconstructed from three indirect references in the outer archive — documents that cite conclusions only derivable from a sustained private study of the sky.

Reference One — administrative correspondence, ~5,800 BCE, author unknown:

“The body’s findings regarding the northern stellar drift are consistent with our own survey data and inconsistent with natural variation. The timeline implied is not one we can act on in the current generation. We can act on it across generations. This is what we are for.”

Reference Two — internal notation on a resource allocation document, ~4,900 BCE:

“Approved. The observatory expansion serves purposes the standard justification does not fully describe. The body is aware of this. The body considers it appropriate.”

Reference Three — marginal note on a Concord relay station maintenance report, ~4,200 BCE, in handwriting that does not match any known Concord administrator:

“Cross-referenced against internal models. Deviation within acceptable range. Monitoring continues.”

(Three documents across sixteen hundred years. The handwriting in the third does not belong to any identified Concord administrator. Someone was reading the Concord’s relay station reports and annotating them for their own records. The annotation is calm. Whatever they found was within acceptable range. The range itself is not defined anywhere in the outer archive.)

V. Administrative Actions

~3,799 BCE — Personnel Transfer Notice, V. Sorath

Complete document. Recovered from the outer archive, personnel records division. One page. Filed without cover documentation.

NOTICE OF PERSONNEL TRANSFER
Subject: V. Sorath, Administrator, Third Forge District
Issued by: Office of Administrative Oversight

V. Sorath is hereby transferred from his current posting effective the date of this notice.

New posting: [not specified]
Transfer duration: [not specified]
Reason for transfer: administrative reassignment

This transfer is authorized under standard personnel reallocation provisions.

Signed: Overseer-Designate T. Cavren, Office of Administrative Oversight

(V. Sorath wrote On the Certainty of the Mandate approximately eight months before this notice was issued. The treatise was circulated internally. It asked, carefully, a question about why the Witness Body maintains records it does not share. It did not press the question. It explicitly declined to press the question. It was filed without comment.)

(Overseer-Designate T. Cavren does not appear in the outer archive before this document. The title “Overseer-Designate” is not a standard administrative title in the Imperium’s personnel hierarchy. Standard transfer notices are issued by the Office of Personnel Administration. This one was not.)

(V. Sorath does not appear in the outer archive after this document. His new posting is blank. He had served the Third Forge District for nineteen years. He had never been transferred before.)

(The transfer was issued eight months after the treatise. The treatise asked a question. The question was not answered.)

~2,700 BCE — Observatory Maintenance Request, Interior Ranges Station Four

Complete document. Recovered from the outer archive, infrastructure records division. Filed under routine maintenance, which is the category it was submitted under.

MAINTENANCE REQUEST — INTERIOR RANGES OBSERVATORY STATION FOUR
Submitted by: Station Administrator K. Thren
Request Classification: Routine Infrastructure

Station Four requires the following materials for scheduled maintenance and operational upkeep:

Relay-grade copper sheeting: approximately 1,400 arm-lengths of rolled sheet, 3 finger-widths thick
Arcane resin, grade A: 34 standard casks (each cask sufficient to seal a door frame on all sides, three times over)
Precision lens assemblies: 17 ground-glass units, each the diameter of a spread hand
Structural binding compound: 9 barrels
Arcane-grade binding compound: 21 barrels
Iron framing, load-bearing: enough to frame a building of four large rooms, floor to ceiling
Stellar calibration instruments, Type 3: 6 units
Deep-sky observation apertures: 4 units

Estimated completion of maintenance: 14 months.
Personnel required: 8 specialists, 12 support staff.
Authorization requested from: Office of Infrastructure Administration.

Approved. — Office of Infrastructure Administration. Date of approval: same day as submission.

(Interior Ranges Observatory Station Four has a recorded floor area roughly equivalent to a large house — four rooms, two floors. The materials requested are sufficient to construct a building three to four times that size from the ground up, not to maintain one that already exists.)

(The request was approved the same day it was submitted. The outer archive contains 4,700 infrastructure requests from this period. The median approval time is eleven days. The fastest prior approval was four days.)

(Station Administrator K. Thren does not appear in any other infrastructure request in the outer archive before or after this one. Stellar calibration instruments and deep-sky observation apertures are not maintenance materials. They are construction materials for a new, significantly larger observation platform.)

(The maintenance was completed in fourteen months, per the follow-up filing. The follow-up filing does not describe what was built. It says only: “Maintenance complete. Station Four operational.” The station’s new capacity is not documented.)

~4,100 BCE — Field Personnel Record, Forge District Seven, Cycle 3 Workers

Complete document. Recovered from the outer archive, personnel records division. One of a very small number of personnel records concerning non-administrative, non-construct workers. The Imperium ran on construct labor. Human workers appear in the record primarily when they filled roles constructs could not — skilled craft, arcane calibration, interpretive judgment. This record covers fourteen of them.

PERSONNEL RECORD — FORGE DISTRICT SEVEN, CYCLE 3
Classification: Non-Construct Labor, Skilled Designation
Filed by: District Labor Coordinator, Office of Personnel Administration

The following individuals are registered as active skilled workers in Forge District Seven for the current cycle. Registration is renewed annually. Workers not renewing registration are considered separated from district service.

Arcane Calibration Specialists (3):
Dorath, M. — 11 years district service. Specialization: relay frequency alignment. Notes: consistently high accuracy. No incidents.
Sorun, T. — 4 years district service. Specialization: construct activation sequencing. Notes: transferred from District Two following restructuring. Performance satisfactory.
Vel, K. — 2 years district service. Specialization: relay frequency alignment. Notes: apprentice designation, under supervision of Dorath, M.

Forge Craft Workers (6):
Arren, P. — 23 years district service. Specialization: precision component finishing, M-class constructs. Notes: longest-serving non-construct worker in current district roster. Has trained four subsequent workers now in district service.
Bael, S. — 8 years district service. Specialization: precision component finishing, L-class constructs. Notes: no incidents.
Ceth, R. — 8 years district service. Specialization: lens and optical assembly. Notes: reassigned from observatory support role following Station Three decommissioning.
Forath, N. — 5 years district service. Specialization: precision component finishing, S-class constructs. Notes: no incidents.
Ilven, M. — 5 years district service. Specialization: structural binding, large-frame constructs. Notes: no incidents.
Thera, J. — 3 years district service. Specialization: lens and optical assembly. Notes: no incidents.

Structural and Physical Labor (5):
Dorn, H. — 16 years district service. Specialization: heavy frame installation. Notes: has requested transfer to District Nine twice. Both requests denied — district retention priority.
Kaen, F. — 9 years district service. Specialization: heavy frame installation. Notes: no incidents.
Orath, L. — 6 years district service. Specialization: material transport, oversized components. Notes: no incidents.
Vorath, S. — 6 years district service. Specialization: material transport, oversized components. Notes: no incidents.
Renvar, C. — 1 year district service. Specialization: general physical labor, unspecified. Notes: new registration this cycle. Origin: transferred from civilian population intake, northern sector.

Filed: Personnel Records, Forge District Seven, Cycle 3. Standard annual renewal.

(Fourteen people. Twenty-three years of service at one end, one year at the other. Arren, P. has been finishing precision components for M-class constructs — the military designation — for twenty-three years. He has trained four of the people who came after him. His name appears in four previous cycle records. The record does not say anything about him beyond his specialization and his years. It does not need to.)

(Dorn, H. has requested transfer twice. Both denied — district retention priority. He has been installing heavy frames for sixteen years. The record notes his transfer requests the way it notes everything else: without comment. He is still there.)

(Ceth, R. was reassigned from observatory support following Station Three’s decommissioning. Station Three does not appear in the outer archive’s infrastructure records as having been decommissioned. It appears in earlier personnel records as active. It does not appear in later ones. Something happened to Station Three. The record of what does not exist in the outer archive.)

(Renvar, C. is listed as transferred from civilian population intake, northern sector. The northern sector in this period was the frontier of the Imperium’s expansion. Civilian population intake is the administrative category for people absorbed into Imperium labor pools from territories brought under control. Renvar, C. has been in the district for one year. The record notes no incidents.)

~3,600 BCE — Operational Closure Notice, Subject: Arven Doth, Trade Liaison

Complete document. Recovered from the outer archive, administrative correspondence division. Filed under contract termination.

NOTICE OF OPERATIONAL CLOSURE
Subject: Arven Doth, Trade Liaison, Concord Relations Office
Issued by: Office of Administrative Oversight
Classification: Internal

Following review of the subject’s recent correspondence and field activity, the Office of Administrative Oversight has determined that continued operational engagement with the subject is not in the Imperium’s interest.

Effective immediately, all active contracts, correspondence chains, and material arrangements involving the subject are to be closed. Personnel currently in contact with the subject through official channels are to cease contact without explanation and redirect any inquiries to the standard contract termination protocol.

The subject’s access to the Concord relations correspondence archive is revoked.

Our operative at the subject’s current location — the relay waystation at the northern Concord approach road, where the subject is lodged for the second night of his return journey — has been notified and is awaiting confirmation of this notice. The subject’s travel documents and personal correspondence are to be secured before the station resumes morning traffic. The subject will not require them.

This notice constitutes full authorization for all necessary actions to effect closure. No further documentation will be issued.

Signed: Overseer-Designate T. Cavren, Office of Administrative Oversight

(Arven Doth was a trade liaison working across the boundary between the Imperium and the Celestial Concord — one of very few individuals with legitimate reason to move freely between both institutions. The outer archive contains no record of what he found on his last journey, or what he reported, or to whom. The “recent correspondence and field activity” that prompted this notice is not described.)

(The operative at the waystation is not named. The notice was sent ahead of the subject’s arrival — it reached the station before he did. It knew his route. It knew which night he would stop there.)

(Arven Doth does not appear in the outer archive after this notice. His correspondence files were not removed — they were integrated smoothly into the existing record, attributed to a successor, as if he had simply been replaced in the ordinary course of administration. The integration was clean enough that it would only be visible to someone who already knew to look for it.)

(Overseer-Designate T. Cavren signed this notice. The same title appears on V. Sorath’s transfer notice two centuries later. Either it passed to a successor who kept the title, or it did not pass at all.)

(The morning traffic resumed on schedule.)

VI. The Mandate Defended

~3,800 BCE — Theological Treatise, Selected Passage

From On the Certainty of the Mandate:

A reader attentive to the structure of this defense will notice that one category of objection is not represented: those concerning what the mandate is ultimately for. Why unification. Why now, across these specific millennia, with this specific urgency. Why the Witness Body maintains records it does not share.

I do not address these objections because I do not have answers that are mine to give. There are things known at levels of this institution that I am not at.

Whether it is also what the Warden asked is a question I have decided not to ask.

V. Sorath was reassigned from the Third Forge District approximately eight months after this treatise was circulated. His subsequent posting is not recorded.

VII. The Advance

~2,400 BCE — Expansion Directive, Standard Form

From Southern Advance Documents:

Indigenous population assessment: nomadic, mammoth-dependent, estimated three to four thousand individuals across the sector. No fixed settlements. No arcane infrastructure. No construct capability. Classification: pre-integration.

Integration assessment will determine whether the indigenous population requires relocation, absorption, or reclassification. Standard protocols apply.

~2,100 BCE — Field Report, Dissenting

From Southern Advance Documents:

They have a word, in their language, that I am told means approximately the line that follows. They use it for the construct advance. They do not use it as a military term. They use it the way one uses a term for weather.

I am reporting that what the constructs are producing in Zone Nine is the end of a people who have been retreating from us for generations and have nowhere left to retreat to.

I do not know what should be done with this report. I have filed it because not filing it seemed worse.

Filing Code: 7-ASH-NINE-SOUTH. No administrator with that code appears in personnel records before or after this date.

(The third document from this period — the one addressed to someone whose name was removed — is not reproduced here. It will not be reproduced here. This is the only editorial decision in this collection that required more than a moment’s consideration.)

VIII. The Silence

~950 BCE — Resource Reallocation Order 7-PRIME

From Era VI Documents:

Production under this reallocation is classified. Classification level: Internal. Access restricted to personnel holding authorization code 7-PRIME or above. Forge Complex Seventeen is placed under direct administration of the Witness Body for the duration of production.

Completion timeline: not specified. Production targets: not specified in this document.

No further orders regarding this reallocation will be issued through standard channels.

Authorization code 7-PRIME does not appear in the standard personnel records. The constructs produced under this order do not appear in any deployment record. They were built. They went somewhere.

~800 BCE — Notice of Trial Suspension

From Era VI Documents:

The Trial of Succession is suspended, effective immediately, for an indefinite period.

The suspension is authorized under the body’s standing authority over trial administration. No further explanation is required or will be provided.

Four words when it resumed, eleven years later: “Proceedings resume. This date.”

Three members before. Four members after. Total membership unchanged. The arithmetic is precise. The identities are not.

IX. The End of the Record

Pre-Year 0 — Fragment, Deep Vault, Provenance Unestablished

From Era VI Documents:

The text begins mid-sentence. The page before it has not been found.

— and we were not wrong about what is coming. We were wrong about the time.

What we did not account for is that the thing we were watching was also watching. We do not know for how long.

We know that something moved in the southern survey zone that none of our constructs produced.

We may not be the only ones who knew.

The vaults will seal when the network fails. This is designed. Whatever comes after will have to find their own way in, and we have made that —

The sentence does not finish. Whether the document does is not known.

X. After

What follows is not a document. It is an observation.

The Machine Wastes, sixty years after Year 0, contains sections that have not been fully surveyed. The autonomous construct activity in those sections is inconsistent with standard emergency-protocol behavior. Standard emergency protocols do not require coordination. What is observed in those sections requires coordination.

Bogrund knows approximately where the Ashen Seat is buried. He has been deciding what to do about that for three years.

Garvak’s excavation logs contain a section that has been deliberately obscured. The method used is not consistent with weather damage.

CR-9 prevents unauthorized excavation in the Wastes with a consistency that exceeds any documented emergency directive.

These three facts are presented without connection. The reader may connect them or decline to.

One further note, appended separately from the rest of this collection:

The filing code on the dissenting field report — 7-ASH-NINE-SOUTH — shares its prefix with the classification code on the resource reallocation order: 7-PRIME. This may be coincidence. The Imperium used numerical prefixes extensively and inconsistently.

It may not be coincidence. If it is not, it suggests the dissenting administrator had access to — or was assigned to — something classified at the 7-level. Which would mean they were not simply a field administrator who noticed something wrong. It would mean they were someone the body had cleared for internal work, who then filed a report the body could not acknowledge without revealing that they knew who had filed it.

The body filed it anyway. The body did not respond. The body reassigned them to a posting that does not appear in the outer archive.

This note is the last entry in this collection because it is the most uncertain, and uncertainty belongs at the end.

This collection is incomplete. Several documents known to exist have not been included. Several documents that should exist have not been found. The gap between those two categories is not small.

Compiled by The Archivist of Starwatch Keep.
